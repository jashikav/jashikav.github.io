FOR PROPERTY MANAGEMENT & CONSTRUCTION

Sound familiar? Let’s get your books on track.

From trust accounts to job costs, your industry has its own bookkeeping challenges. Here’s how focused support can help.

01

Mixing personal money with trust and operating accounts

The problem: Tenant security deposits, owner trust funds, and operating cash need careful separation. Construction adds project advances, client deposits, and owner draws to the mix. One swipe of the wrong debit card can create tangled records and compliance concerns.

The fix: A structured chart of accounts and monthly reconciliation of escrow, trust, and operating accounts help keep funds separate and flag transactions that need attention.

02

Job costing and tracking work in progress

The problem: A money-in, money-out total doesn’t tell you whether a build or renovation is profitable. Labor, materials, subcontractor invoices, and change orders need to be tracked against each job. Without accurate work in progress (WIP) tracking, cost overruns can stay hidden until it’s too late.

The fix: Map receipts and contractor payments to specific jobs or property units, and keep WIP records current for a clearer view of gross margins as each project progresses.

03

Multi-entity books and owner statement headaches

The problem: Managing properties for multiple owners—or separate LLCs for each property—can turn your books into a tangled pile. Owners need transparent monthly statements showing their rental income, repair deductions, and management fees.

The fix: Maintain separate books for each entity, with property sub-ledgers and class tracking where appropriate, so monthly owner reports are organized and easier to prepare.

04

Subcontractor 1099 traps at tax time

The problem: Construction and property maintenance rely on independent contractors, from painters and electricians to handymen. Chasing W-9s in January and sorting out which payments need reporting makes tax season a stressful scramble.

The fix: Build W-9 collection into contractor onboarding before payment, and track payments and potential 1099 reporting needs throughout the year so your records are ready for tax preparation.
